Breaking Up With Goodreads? Exploring Book Tracking Alternatives

I love tracking what I read and have always been doing so ever since I developed a passion for reading. Ever since I started reading and tracking my reads, I have done it using the good old Goodreads. But now, every time I scroll through the bookish community on the internet, I hear that people are BREAKING UP WITH GOODREADS, and so that got me wondering what all the fuss was about with the use of the app and what alternatives readers were using to track the books they were reading.

That’s when I decided to do some research of my own and explore some of the other alternatives available to track my reading. Here are some of the alternatives that I found and which worked for me.

But before I get started, here’s a little about what a book tracking app or site is for those new to the bookish community. If you’re already familiar, feel free to read ahead.

## What is a Book tracking app or site?

A book tracking app or site, simply put, is an online system for book readers to organize and manage all the books they have read or plan to read in the future. These apps or sites can sometimes be community-based or can simply serve as a portable library.

## Storygraph

Storygraph is a close competitor to Goodreads, founded by a black woman. The app is free to sign up for, but it also offers a paid version for a more personalized experience. Like Goodreads, Storygraph allows you to track, rate and review books in pretty much the same manner. However, besides the normal tracking, rating and reviewing of books, the app offers its users a ton of other features for a better tracking experience.

What it has:

● Personalized recommendations based on your reading preferences and mood.
● Tracking of your reading data with statistics charts and graphs.
● Ability for users to rate books with quarter and half stars.
● Built-in DNF (Did Not Finish) and owned shelves, with a reading journal for note-making.
● Ability to mark books with content or trigger warnings.
● Ability to scan the barcode on books to add them to a shelf.
● Set yearly reading goals and page goals.
● Users can import their book shelve from goodreads with ease.
● Other interactive features like buddy reads, read-alongs, Bookclubs, prompt-based reading challenges and Giveaways.

What it lacks:

● Difficulty in navigation for first-time users.
● Lack of a more personalized profile page.
● Lacks an interactive social community.
