Breaking Up With Goodreads? Exploring Book Tracking Alternatives

I love tracking what I read and have always been doing so ever since I developed a passion for reading. Ever since I started reading and tracking my reads, I have done it using the good old Goodreads. But now, every time I scroll through the bookish community on the internet, I hear that people are BREAKING UP WITH GOODREADS, and so that got me wondering what all the fuss was about with the use of the app and what alternatives readers were using to track the books they were reading.

That’s when I decided to do some research of my own and explore some of the other alternatives available to track my reading. Here are some of the alternatives that I found and which worked for me.

But before I get started, here’s a little about what a book tracking app or site is for those new to the bookish community. If you’re already familiar, feel free to read ahead.

## What is a Book tracking app or site?

A book tracking app or site, simply put, is an online system for book readers to organize and manage all the books they have read or plan to read in the future. These apps or sites can sometimes be community-based or can simply serve as a portable library.

## Storygraph

Storygraph is a close competitor to Goodreads, founded by a black woman. The app is free to sign up for, but it also offers a paid version for a more personalized experience. Like Goodreads, Storygraph allows you to track, rate and review books in pretty much the same manner. However, besides the normal tracking, rating and reviewing of books, the app offers its users a ton of other features for a better tracking experience.

<b>What it has:</b>

-  Personalized recommendations based on your reading preferences and mood.
-  Tracking of your reading data with statistics charts and graphs.
-  Ability for users to rate books with quarter and half stars.
-  Built-in DNF (Did Not Finish) and owned shelves, with a reading journal for note-making.
-  Ability to mark books with content or trigger warnings.
-  Ability to scan the barcode on books to add them to a shelf.
-  Set yearly reading goals and page goals.
-  Users can import their book shelve from goodreads with ease.
-  Other interactive features like buddy reads, read-alongs, Bookclubs, prompt-based reading challenges and Giveaways.

<b>What it lacks:</b>

-  Difficulty in navigation for first-time users.
-  Lack of a more personalized profile page.
-  Lacks an interactive social community.

## Bookmory

Bookmory is an app that provides its users with a more private and personal tracking experience. The app is free to sign up for but offers premium features at a subscription cost.

<b>What it has:</b>

- Effortless tracking system with book and page tracking.
- Ability to track your reading on a daily basis with note-making to remember key points.
- Timer to set and achieve your daily reading goals.
- Statistics based on your reading preferences.
- Stylish reading calendar and note-making that you can easily share with your friends on social media and other platforms.
- Allows user to rate books on half star rating.
- Allows to sort books into various collections and wish lists.
- Easy migration of data from Goodreads

<b>What it lacks:</b>

- No social community feature.
- Absence of a personal profile page.
- Lack of a web page.

## Bookworm Reads

Bookworm is a community based book tracking app which is fun and colourful. The app was founded by Amelia. It’s free to sign up but for more features users can subscribe to their pro-worm subscription. Join if you are ready to be dress up like a worm. Sounds like fun!

<b>What it has:</b>

- Normal book tracking system.
- Personal Profile and feed page
- Growing and interactive social community.
- Gives users a colourful and very playful tracking experience.
- Allows users to create or join different Bookclubs.
- Allows user to rate books on half star rating.
- Allow users to import their reading data Goodreads.

<b>What it lacks:</b>

- The app looks very playful and lack seriousness for a reader who reads a lot and writes serious reviews.
- Users may face bugs and the App crashes frequently
- A worm in place a users profile picture makes it lack human touch.
- Takes a few days to import your data from goodreads.
- Web page has a limited version, users need to download the app for a better experience.
- A lots of book covers are missing which cannot be updated by users.

## Literal

Literal is another unpopular community based book tracking app, which lets users track their books in a similar way as compared to the other apps or sites. Free to sign up and doesn’t offer a subscription plan.

<b>What it has:</b>

- Easy navigation
- Recommends books on your previous reading preferences.
- Helps pick your next read and add customisable book shelves.
- Social community so you never read alone
- User can create or join Bookclubs.
- Easy import of data from Goodreads.
- Half star ratings

<b>What it lacks:</b>

- Does have a social but not a very interactive one. You can leave comments and likes on other users reviews.
- Can’t add trigger warnings.
- Lack popularity.

## Goodreads

Goodreads is an app or site that allows you to track all that you read into categories like read, currently reading, and want to read. It’s free to sign up and has no features hidden under a subscription fee, making it budget-friendly for users.

Some of the other features of Goodreads include:

<b>What it has:</b>

- Rating and reviewing the books you have read, as well as reading and interacting with other reviewers by leaving likes and comments.
- Adding personalized shelves and tags to your existing bookshelf.
- Ability to hide or make public your profile page.
- Access to a huge community to interact with through groups and book clubs.
- Book recommendations and news about upcoming book releases, as well as seasonal book recommendations through Discovery.
- Browsing books through different categories.
- Setting your reading goal in the form of a reading challenge.
- Has a huge collection of books and author profile which you can interact with.

<b>What it lacks:</b>

- The biggest lack of Goodreads i.e it has not been updated ever since it was taken over by Amazon.
- The app and website work differently, there is not much a user can do through the app.
- Can’t rate books with half stars and trigger warnings.

## Conclusion

Though, these are some of the alternatives I have found, tried, and tested, I still find myself drawn back to Goodreads time and again. I love the efficient tracking system and the community of book lovers that Goodreads has to offer. Over the years, I have made many friends and discovered many new authors through Goodreads. So, my verdict is that I am not breaking up with Goodreads anytime soon.
