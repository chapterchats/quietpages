## My Review

<b>Actually a 3.5 stars for me</b>

I’m so glad NetGalley dropped this book into my inbox at just the right time. I had recently finished reading Hot Chocolate on Thursday with my book club, thoroughly enjoyed it, and was looking forward to reading Matcha on Monday as well.

Matcha on Monday is the second installment in the Marble Café series. Like the first book, it follows 12 bittersweet, heartwarming stories, each representing a month of the year. We also get to meet a few familiar characters from Hot Chocolate on Thursday and learn more about their lives. And if you liked the Maestro , you’re sure to enjoy seeing more of him in this book.

The stories follow a similar path of interconnected stories, where small day-to-day chance encounters leave a meaningful impact on our lives. The book once again begins at the cozy Marble Café, only this time with a cup of matcha rather than hot chocolate.

From a personal viewpoint, I preferred Hot Chocolate on Thursday over this book. The stories here didn’t quite stand out for me emotionally and failed to leave a lasting impact. I also found some of them to be rather lengthy.

I’m unable to choose a favorite story, as none of them truly struck a chord with me. However, if I had to pick the ones I enjoyed the most, they would be:
3. Sparrows in Early Spring
7. The Old Man and the Tanzaku Strips
8. Finding the Missing Volume

Overall, as Matcha on Monday offers the same cozy atmosphere and storytelling, it didn’t resonate with me as deeply as Hot chocolate on Thursday.
