## My Review

Hot Chocolate on Thursday by Michiko Aoyama is the first installment in the Marble Café series. Set in a quiet neighborhood in Tokyo, with seasonal cherry blossoms lining the river, sits the Marble Café, offering us glimpses into the lives of its regular customers. The story opens with a girl ordering her favorite hot chocolate at this cozy café in Tokyo, from where the stories travels all the way to Sydney, Australia, and eventually comes a full circle back to the Marble Café, where the girl once again orders her favorite hot chocolate.

Hot Chocolate on Thursday is a moving collection of 12 interconnected stories that show how small, everyday acts can change the way we view and live our lives. While the book doesn’t offer concrete solutions to life’s problems, it encourages us to pause and reflect on their own lives.

All 12 stories, each represented by a color, leave behind meaningful messages that can warm your heart, bring a smile to your face, and sometimes even a tear to your eye. The stories I connected with the most were Stories 3, 4, and 9. I related to the 3rd and 4th story on a personal level because, as a kindergarten teacher, I understand how something as simple as nail polish can become an unspoken rule or distraction in class. I also completely agreed with Yasuko’s thoughts on how people often underestimate a teacher’s job, assuming all we do is sing, dance, and play with children, when in reality there is so much more to it. As for the 9th story, I simply loved its vibe—it brought a smile to my face and completely cast its spell on me.

However, despite being a comforting and cozy read, it didn’t fully meet the expectations created by all the hype surrounding it.
