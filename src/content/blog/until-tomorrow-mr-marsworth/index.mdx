Adjusting to life in her parents' Midwestern hometown isn't easy, but once Reenie takes up a paper route with her older brother Dare, she has something she can look forward to. As they introduce themselves to every home on their route, Reenie's stumped by just one--the house belonging to Mr. Marsworth, the town recluse. When a BookTuber recommended Until Tomorrow,Mr. Marsworth, it immediately caught my attention, and her praise for the book turned out to be completely justified.

## Settings: 
Set in the year 1968 in Lake Liberty against the backdrop of the Vietnam War, where young American men struggled with the possibility of enlisting or being drafted to fight a war they believed to be wrong.

## Plot: 
Until Tomorrow, Mr. Marsworth follows the story of 11-year-old Reenie Kelly, who, since her mother died, has moved to Lake Liberty along with her two brothers to live with their Gram. Their father is in North Dakota working to save money to send his eldest son Billy to college so that he does not have to fight in the war. Meanwhile, in Lake Liberty, Reenie Kelly is going all out to save her brother from the draft and fulfill her mother’s dreams. Reenie Kelly has landed herself a paper route and has found herself a pen pal, a reclusive shut-in, Mr. Marsworth, with whom she starts exchanging letters.

## Characters: 
The main characters of this book, Mr. Marsworth and Reenie Kelly, kept me engaged. Our main character Reenie is a stubborn and impatient 11-year-old, who does a lot of stupid things throughout the story, like writing letters to her brother's college or to the President of the United States, appealing to him to end the war. However, she means no harm; all she wants is to see her brother Billy Kelly safe and forever with her. On the other hand, Mr. Marsworth is a recluse who tries his best to guide young Kelly in the right direction through his letters. He cheers her on when she is sad, shares in her happiness when she is happy, and gets mad at her for doing stupid things. The other characters we meet through the book are Billy, Dare, Reenie's two brothers, and Gram. Carl Grace, Mr. Marsworth’s assistant. Rat and Cutler, kid is Lake Liberty who were all out to bully to Kelly kids.

## My Thoughts: 
Sometimes funny, sometimes heartbreaking, I loved reading this book. The epistolary format felt very original to me; I have never read a book told entirely in letters. Author Sheila O’Connor's writing is beautiful. Each letter that Reenie Kelly writes to Mr. Marsworth is “STUPENDOUS” (just like Reen could say). This book highlights the fear of losing a loved one to war and shows the extent to which a sister will go to keep her brother from getting drafted and how one should have the right to choose whether a war is right or wrong. This is not a typical war story as it focuses more on relationships, friendships, and strong family bonds and values despite past secrets. The only part of this book that left me a little disappointed was that ending, I felt it ended abruptly, leaving me with a sense of want more.

## Will I Recommend this book: 
Yes, I highly recommend this book. If you are looking to add more historical fiction to your TBR, you should definitely add this one. While this is a middle-grade novel, it deals with themes of war, loss, and uncertainty that may be emotional for some children, especially those with family members serving in the military.
