## My Review

With all the hype surrounding Strange Pictures, curiosity eventually got the better of me—and before I knew it, I was reading it… only to end up regretting it.

The story itself has a lot of potential and, at its core, is actually quite interesting. However, the translation didn’t seem to do the book any justice. One thing I kept wondering while reading was why this book was categorized as horror. There was nothing particularly creepy or terrifying about it.

The pictures, which should have been the most important element of the book—especially considering the title—felt like an afterthought. Instead of being strange or unsettling, most of them were simple, childlike drawings or timelines referring to the events leading up to the murders. There was really nothing that unusual about them.

I’m honestly not sure who is to blame here—the original work or the translation. The writing in the translation felt off and often went in circles, repeating explanations of things we had already understood. This made the book feel unnecessarily long.

In the end, while the concept behind Strange Pictures is undeniably unique, the overall execution left me disappointed. It might still appeal to readers who enjoy unconventional mysteries, but sadly it wasn’t the reading experience I had hoped for.

Will I be reading the other two books? It will take quite a bit of convincing for me to pick them up.
